Finding community on a college campus

On a college campus, discovering where you belong can make the difference between feeling lost and feeling at home.

By Gabriella Palamidessi | May 13, 2026

Walking onto a college campus for the first time, students can be surrounded by thousands of people and still feel alone. This shows why belonging matters: feeling secure, accepted and included. Belonging does not emerge from being around others, but from how people actually engage with a group and communicate.

On college campuses, a student's sense of belonging can shape academic success and mental health, making it essential. According to a study by the National Library of Medicine, feeling a sense of belonging may lead students to engage more deeply with their studies, leading to persistence and success.

Cal Poly's Swing Dance Club

The Cal Poly Swing Dance Club has been bringing students together since 1998 with weekly lessons open to all experience levels. More than just a dance club, it's a welcoming community where students can meet new people and learn to swing dance.

Cal Poly Swing Dance Club hosts social dances and lessons throughout the year, introducing students and community members to swing dancing.

Dancers of all experience levels are welcome to the dance floor to learn and practice various swing dance styles, including Lindy Hop, East Coast swing, Balboa, and more.

Emma Stanciu, a first-year biology major, is new to the world of swing dancing but encourages everyone to get on the dance floor and try it.

Not everyone at the event came to dance; many came just to watch. One visitor described swing dancing as “generational” and something special to witness.

Guests arrived in everything from jeans and T-shirts to vintage-inspired outfits, blending casual style with classic swing flair.

When entering college, many students struggle to find their people and often experience loneliness. For many, the challenge begins with the transition into a completely new environment.

“Moving away from home creates a challenging transition for many students,” said Noah Chalfin, a college counselor and first-year students coordinator at Cal Poly San Luis Obispo. “I think it can kind of interfere with one's ability to develop that sense of belonging.”

Chalfin believes that it's important for students to find where they fit in. “I think having that sense of belonging is going to support improved academic performance, and overall an improved mental health,” he said, “it helps support a healthy social engagement and interaction.”

“As an RA, it is very important to help residents feel like they belong,” said Jocelyn Penaloza, an RA for the Sierra Madre dorm at Cal Poly. “It is challenging because every individual is different and has different needs, but I try to make small interactions meaningful and make events that interest them.”

Penaloza explains that it can be challenging to deal with students who feel homesick or who don't want to participate at times, but she continues to encourage them to explore new opportunities.

“I personally have had one-on-one conversations with my residents, trying to encourage them to join clubs, organizations, and just socialize,” she said, “but it is up to the residents to step out of their comfort zone.”

Finding a sense of belonging in college is a gradual process that can significantly impact a student's academic success and well-being. Chalfin wants students to recognize that it takes time to find where they belong.

“I think that for some students, finding a sense of belonging does take more than one year on a college campus,” he said, “It's important to understand that it's an ongoing process, and you have to be patient and open-minded to new opportunities.”
